Dependent-samples (paired) t-tests

Purpose

In this assignment, you will compare the use of human-powered transport and public transit by calculating a paired samples t-test (sometimes called a dependent samples t-test) and a confidence interval for the difference of the means, and then you will interpret your findings. To learn more about the climate change impacts of transportation choices, see the links below.

Comparing the Use of Human-Powered Transport and Public Transit

When they completed the Climate Change Action Inventory (Barchard, Okagawa, Hoffman, & Odents, 2021), participants were asked how frequently they use human-powered transport and how often they used public transit. The possible response options were:

1 = Less than once a year

2 = Once a year

3 = Several times a year

4 = Once a month

5 = A couple times a month

6 = Once a week

7 = At least 3 times a week

8 = At least 7 times a week

9 = At least 14 times a week

You will conduct a paired samples t-test to test the null hypothesis that the mean difference in the frequency of human-powered transit and public transit is 0 (\(𝐻_0: \mu_𝐷 = 0\)) vs the alternative that the mean difference is not zero (\(𝐻_1: \mu_D \ne 0\)).

  1. Calculate a paired samples t-test for human-powered transport (CCAI_Transportation4) and public transit (CCAI_Transportation5). Put the first variable in the left-hand side of the Paired Variables window, and the second in the right.

  2. Request the Student’s t test.

  3. Under Additional Statistics, ask for Descriptives and the 95% confidence interval for the Mean Difference.
